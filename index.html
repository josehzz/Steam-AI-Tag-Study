DATA • ACQUIRING THE DATA

How This Data Was Built

  • Data comes from Steam public game pages, I built a scrapper using Claude to get relevant data like:

    • Name, Tags, Review Count, Review Score, Review Description, Release Date, Base Price (USD) and AI Disclosure

  • I placed all the data inside a .csv, that I then cleaned by:

    • Finding empty/null entries on review_count column and setting them to 0
    • Finding empty/null entries on review_score column and setting them to 0.0
    • Finding empty/null entries on review_desc column and setting them to "0 user reviews"

  • To process the data inside the AI disclosure tag I used Gemini to set up agents that would read the contents of each entry that has a disclosure and classify that into a few categories (a single game can have multiple):

    • Visual: Game has art assets made with AI
    • Audio: Game has audio assets made with AI
    • Text: Game has text generated with AI
    • Code: Game has code generated with AI
    • Prototyping: Some assets were generated as placeholder to help with production and development of the game
    • Live_Generated: Some assets inside the game are generated while playing
    • Marketing: Some assets were generated with AI for marketing purposes (Capsule art, Promotional Images and Videos, Social Media Campaings, etc...)
    • External_Ai_service: Game links to an AI service (like Claude, Gemini, OpenAI)
    • Declares_No_Ai: Game features no AI generated content
    • Unspecified: Some disclosures say they may have AI generated content but don't specify how or where

  • Then using Gemini I made this dashboard and a few graphs to help make sense of this data

  • Turned most of the data into .json so it is easier to graph most things

  • Audited Gemini's multi-label tags against 300 randomly sampled disclosures tagged manually (87.7% Micro F1, 77.3% exact match — see the Validation & Accuracy tab below).

DATA • CAVEATS

Limitations

  • There are some games released before Jan-2024 that added the AI disclosure tag not included in this dataset

  • Some games can feature content made by players, these games often say they don't use AI assets by players might do so. This was classified as Declares_No_Ai.

  • This data was collected between Nov-02-26 and Nov-08-26, so recently released games stats might vary alot.

  • AI Disclosures are self reported meaning that there might be games that did not disclosure their AI use, I think most developers are truthful but in practice the true amount of AI usage must be a little bit more

  • In the 300-game manual audit, core modality tags (Visual, Audio, Marketing, Code, Text) achieved 83.6%–94.7% F1, while Prototyping (20.6% recall) and Unspecified (28.6% recall) are conservative lower bounds in the full dataset.

  • Price & Mature-Content Confounding: AI-tagged games skew heavily toward low price tiers (avg $6.03 vs $7.90) and mature-flagged titles (30.36% AI share). Rather than filtering out or down-weighting cheap entries, the Price-Stratified view in Is it Relevant? keeps all games intact and pairs within-tier comparisons with a multivariate OLS/logistic regression controlling for price, age, mature content, and top 20 tags.

Common Failure Modes Spotted (n = 300)

68 mismatches analyzed

  • 1. Under-tagging Prototyping (27 of 34 missed • 20.6% Recall): When disclosures describe AI used "during development", temporary demo copy (Hostile Takeover, Inc.), or hybrid pipelines where AI drafts are manually edited (A Night With: Spirit, Esports Godfather), the classifier usually applied only the final asset modality (Visual/Text) and omitted Prototyping.

  • 2. Over-tagging on negations, vague wording & classic game AI (17 FP Text, 15 FP Visual, 9 FP Audio, 5 FP Live_Generated): Negated clauses ("does not use AI to generate images"), generic statements ("some assets" guessed as Visual instead of Unspecified, missing 10 of 14), and non-generative bot AI ("Enemy Bots and Turrets are A.I. Powered" tagged Live_Generated) produced the bulk of false positives.

STUDY SYNTHESIS // FINAL FINDINGS

Conclusions & Key Developer Takeaways

📈
FINDING 01 // MARKET ADOPTION

The Disclosure Surge vs. Discovery Bottleneck

Generative AI disclosure in new Steam games exploded from 1.5% in January 2024 to 47.2% by late 2026, yet AI games shrink from 35.6% of zero-review titles to 9.48% of games reaching 1,000+ reviews. Even after controlling for price tier, game age, mature content, and top 20 tags, AI titles still have 41.6% lower odds of reaching 10+ reviews (OR = 0.584, 95% CI [0.559, 0.610]).

⏱️
FINDING 02 // VELOCITY & TRUST

The 18.5% Review Velocity Friction

Non-AI games reach Steam's critical 10-review visibility threshold in an average of 142.1 days, whereas AI games require 168.4 days — an 18.5% delay. While hit AI games prove players will embrace great titles regardless of disclosure, the average AI game faces greater community skepticism and slower word-of-mouth.

🏷️
FINDING 03 // PRICING & CONFOUNDING

Not Just Cheap Shovelware (-4.07 pts Controlled)

AI games average $6.03 vs $7.90 (-23.7%) and peak at 28.1% share in $0.01–$4.99, but budget mix is not the whole story. The score gap persists inside every price tier (including -4.29 pts in $15–$29.99). Controlling for price, age, mature content, and genre via OLS leaves a -4.07 pt score penalty (95% CI [-4.60, -3.55]) vs -4.96 pts raw.

🧩
FINDING 04 // GENRE DIVERGENCE

The Multiplayer vs. Solo Divide (33.3% Similarity)

Steam's biggest review drivers — Multiplayer (#3), Co-op (#6), Open World (#7), and Online Co-Op (#10) — account for over 24 million reviews, but developers rarely disclose AI in them (AI ranks #32 to #92). Generative AI remains heavily siloed in solo, casual, and 2D games.

⚠️
FINDING 05 // DEVELOPER TRAP

The "Declares No AI" Self-Inflicted Tag

Over 116 developers typed "We do NOT use AI" into Steam's AI disclosure form. Because Steam's system flags any game with text in that field, these games received the AI disclosure badge anyway — subjecting them to automated filters, user skepticism, and browser extensions designed to blur AI content.
